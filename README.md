**Different photosynthetic responses to heat and light favour green and red over brown macroalgae in the Mediterranean Sea**

-----

**Citation**

Hesse, L., Weiss, M., Mezger, S. D., El-Khaled, Y. C., Müller, B., Kler Lago, A., Schwarzmeier, M., & Wild, C. (2025). Different photosynthetic responses to heat and light favour green and red over brown macroalgae in the Mediterranean Sea. Scientific Reports. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-28235-8

**Abstract**

Coastal Mediterranean Sea ecosystems dominated by macroalgae of different phylogenetic origins can undergo drastic changes due to ocean warming. This study experimentally tested the combined effect of temperature (12, 22, and 30 °C) and light (dim, ambient, and bright) on photosynthetic and respiratory activity in three common macroalgae from Giglio Island, Italy belonging to distinct taxonomic groups (green, red and brown). Results showed that the green and red taxa increased their net photosynthesis by up to 93% and 113% under ambient light and 22 °C, while the brown macroalga increased its net photosynthesis by only 41% under the same conditions. Net photosynthesis of all taxa declined at 30 °C, most substantially for the brown taxon (−89% in dim light). Furthermore, the green and red taxa benefitted strongly from bright light, while the brown taxon showed evidence of photoinhibition. The overall photosynthesis-to-respiration ratios across treatments remained positive for the green and red taxa but declined below 1 for the brown taxon at 30 °C. These findings suggest that green and red macroalgae are more resilient to the interacting effects of ocean warming and light availability than brown macroalgae, with potential implications for future community composition in Mediterranean coastal ecosystems.
